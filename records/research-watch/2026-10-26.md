## Findings and next investigations

The proposed [bibliography changes](../../related-work.md) include the detailed comparisons and review qualifications.

### 1. A-SAW: maintained architectural artifacts under mandatory review

Klatt, Di Grazia, and Schneider, [arXiv:2610.13647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13647), submitted **9 October 2026** and newly found during the overlap. Inspected primary metadata and selected full-text sections on workflow design, evaluation, results, boundary conditions, and threats.

**Implication:** A-SAW is direct prior art for persistent, versioned architectural representations that agents draft and humans govern. It offers concrete comparison dimensions for PR #5's proposed architectural-practice direction: catalog-grounded referents, explicit unknowns, intended-versus-implemented traceability, and where human validation concentrates. Its industrial evaluation supports feasibility of structured regeneration, while its confounds and quality–trust gap preclude treating polished artifacts or rubric gains as correctness or human understanding. A bounded PostCode investigation should compare these properties rather than claim architectural representation itself as novel.

### 2. Code of Theseus: design information changes structure, not measured behavior

Sas et al., [arXiv:2610.20068v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20068v2), submitted **14 October** and revised **15 October 2026**. Inspected metadata and selected full text on representation, generation, judging, round-trip reconstruction, results, discussion, and threats; did not run the [released artifacts](https://github.com/SasCezar/code-theseus).

**Implication:** the round trip separates two outcomes PostCode should not collapse. Recovered requirements and architecture made an agent's module set closer to the original but did not significantly increase held-out test success. This suggests a useful evaluation pattern for examining whether a human-facing architectural representation preserves behavioral evidence, structural organization, or both. The study evaluates agent reconstruction and model-judged artifacts, not human comprehension, design quality, or historically true intent.

### 3. ABRA: interactive representations as a shared communication surface

Kim and Kim, [arXiv:2610.30780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30780), submitted **22 October 2026**. Inspected the primary full text on the interaction model, prototype, demonstration, experiments, results, and limitations, plus the linked prototype identity.

**Implication:** ABRA provides close conceptual prior art for moving beyond chat by allowing humans and agents to manipulate shared task-specific representations with published interaction meanings. PostCode should distinguish representation content, referent identity, interaction semantics, and the authority or evidence behind an agent-resolved consequence. ABRA's experiments establish only that selected models can author and invoke the canvas protocol in one-turn tasks; human interpretation and software-development usefulness remain untested. A small architecture task comparing chat, generated static views, and an explicitly bound interactive view would be informative.

### 4. Decision provenance can differ from account identity

Manji, [arXiv:2610.28683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28683), submitted **19 October 2026** after the previous watch. Inspected the primary full text on repository records, exact-revision gate reconstruction, action traces, findings, and validity threats.

**Implication:** an approval attached to a human account does not establish that the human made the decision. For PostCode's supervision and observation model, actor, credential, decider, inspected evidence, and exact repository revision should remain separate. No interface can infer an unrecorded private decision; qualification should expose that limit. The study's single-startup, participant-author setting and incomplete audit/transcript coverage rule out causal or prevalence claims.

### 5. Ferris and Bonilla: full-text evidence for non-programmer supervision

Ferris and Bonilla, [arXiv:2610.08651](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08651v1), submitted **6 October 2026** and already listed from abstract-only review. The now-accessible full text was inspected across the case, workflow, control boundaries, assurance failures, discussion, and limitations.

**Implication:** the paper is more directly relevant than the existing entry recorded. It describes one operator without formal software-engineering training governing a production platform through plans, persistent rules, staging, checks, reviewing agents, access boundaries, and deployment decisions rather than exhaustive source review. It also records failures in the supervisory evidence itself. This is useful boundary-case evidence for the premise strengthened in PR #5, but not proof that non-programmers generally can practice software architecture or that the workflow reduced defects. A PostCode comparison should ask what the person can judge directly, what is known only through generated evidence, and how evidence scope and action authority are presented.
