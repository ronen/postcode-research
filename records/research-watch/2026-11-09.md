## Findings and bounded next investigations

The proposed [bibliography changes](../../related-work.md) contain the detailed comparisons and qualifications.

### 1. Persistent collaboration needs governed acts, provenance, and compaction survival

Granitzer et al., [arXiv:2611.00689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00689), submitted **31 October 2026**. Inspected primary metadata and selected full-text sections on architecture, controlled experiments, field operation, lessons, instrument failures, and threats.

**Analytic level:** collaborative-practice design knowledge and field evidence; not a human architectural-interface study.

**Implication:** the controlled and field material shows why a shared environment should represent who requested, decided, authorized, and completed work; distinguish enforced rules from conversational instructions; retain attributable records; and make critical state survive compaction. Human attention and decision latency should be measured as first-class outcomes rather than hidden behind agent throughput. A bounded PostCode construction episode can test whether architectural questions, qualifications, approvals, and unresolved obligations remain inspectable after context compression or hand-off.

### 2. Execution traces can improve bounded oversight without becoming architectural truth

Yan et al., [arXiv:2611.04466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04466), submitted **3 November 2026**. Inspected primary metadata and selected full-text sections on interviews, system design, controlled and expert studies, benchmark results, and limitations.

**Analytic levels:** direct human investigation and collaborative-practice mechanisms; the agent benchmark does not establish human effects.

**Implication:** VibeTrace provides a comparison for selective inspection, intervention from a prior state, preservation of validated progress, and provenance-bearing reuse. PostCode should compare an execution trace with a qualified architectural account on questions that require both process history and current structure. Short comprehension tasks and experienced coding-agent users do not establish sustained architectural practice or source-independent usefulness.

### 3. Visual support can broaden review while creating false relationship semantics

Parveen et al., [arXiv:2611.05437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05437), submitted **3 November 2026**. Inspected primary metadata and selected full-text sections on design, results, relationship misinterpretation, discussion, and threats.

**Analytic level:** direct human investigation in requirements review, with design implications for account presentation.

**Implication:** the study distinguishes additional source-supported observations, changed decisions, and stronger rationales, which are useful separate outcomes for PostCode. Misread proximity and traceability links make relationship meaning, source context, and incomplete coverage concrete parts of the epistemological contract. The fixed-order design prevents attributing observed changes to the visualization, and requirements review is not architectural practice.

### 4. Retained intent needs qualification beyond prompt-to-code lineage

Albu et al., [arXiv:2611.09395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09395), submitted **6 November 2026**. Inspected primary metadata and selected full-text sections on Tasklets, mapping and history, decision-record export, design iterations, participant evidence, and limitations.

**Analytic levels:** architectural-account mechanism and formative design evidence; weak direct-human evidence.

**Implication:** Tracybot is close mechanism-level prior art for connecting agent conversations to changed implementation and preserving historical lineage. PostCode should distinguish communicated intent, human-authored rationale, implementation effect, current validity, and authority rather than treating a prompt as the decision itself. The screenshot-based survey does not show that the representation improves understanding or architectural decisions.

### 5. Human-curated tours are a constructive comparator for persistent partial understanding

Storey, [arXiv:2611.08456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08456), submitted **5 November 2026**. Inspected the primary paper's design goals, interactions, self-use episode, risks, and evaluation agenda.

**Analytic levels:** constructive account-quality and collaborative-practice design knowledge; no established human-effect claim.

**Implication:** Cognitive Tours makes goals, decisions, code, tests, open questions, and possible staleness part of a revisable human-curated route. It offers a direct comparison for PostCode's retained partial accounts and for the proposed division between machine suggestions and human verification. A bounded comparison should measure maintenance burden, stale-account detection, recovered context, and decision quality against capable chat with diagrams and notes.

### 6. Repository maps help agents conditionally, according to the task bottleneck

Sepidband et al., [arXiv:2611.07286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07286), submitted **5 November 2026**. Inspected primary metadata and selected full-text sections on workflows, benchmarks, results, behavioral analysis, discussion, and threats.

**Analytic level:** agent-facing account behavior and possible analysis infrastructure; no direct human evidence.

**Implication:** AST, call-graph, dependency, documentation, and hybrid maps changed agent search, token use, and task outcomes in task-dependent ways. This supports selecting a lens based on the current question and testing whether the represented relations match the bottleneck. Because the treatment bundles the maps with steering and a map-first hook, and benchmark outcomes do not establish human usefulness or account fidelity, any reuse assessment should separate representation content, access protocol, agent behavior, and human outcomes.
